05

Research gap

When is changing the allocation worth it?

Prior art already supplies the pieces: model selection, adaptive handoff, session continuity, and context transfer. The question here is when execution evidence justifies revising those choices, and whether the revision helps the whole task.

One research direction

Use checkpoint evidence to decide when—and how—to reallocate.

Allocation means the single agent’s model, its evidence packet, and its supported reasoning/token budget—all under the session’s resource limits.

The gap to test: can a checkpoint controller preserve task completion while lowering episode cost within a latency budget, beyond matched request-level, history-aware per-call, fixed-allocation, and composed-policy baselines?

For example: a visible test failure might call for better context, a model change, more reasoning budget, or simply continuing the current plan. The failure alone does not tell us which response helps. The same allocation question also applies to ordinary progress and planned transitions—not just recovery.

One argument, three connected steps

Execution evidence → allocation choice → episode value.

  1. 01 · Execution evidence

    Does new evidence change the right choice?

    Repository discoveries, tool results, and progress can inform the next allocation. Test their value beyond the opening request; compare checkpoint timing with an equally informed per-call policy allowed to stay.

  2. 02 · Allocation choice

    Which feasible combination should run next?

    A compact packet may change the preferred model, and a model switch may change how much history is worth sending. Test model–context interactions, rather than assuming independently tuned choices compose well. Context capacity and the evidence actually sent remain distinct.

  3. 03 · Episode value

    Does the choice earn its full cost?

    Measure alternative continuations from the same checkpoint and remaining resources, then test the frozen policy in fresh episodes. Judge final success, spend, and wall time—not risk or local progress alone. Charge routing, context/cache transfer, re-reading, and failed attempts.

What would support the hypothesis?

Lower total episode cost at a predeclared task-completion margin and latency budget, against strong baselines with matched evidence, pool, scaffold, and training resources. The integrated phase must show additional value beyond independently tuned, composed component policies. The evaluation page defines the evaluation.

What would argue against it?

If an equally informed per-call policy with a stay action, fixed allocation, or simple composition matches the learned policy after all overhead, the corresponding learned complexity is not justified. Sparse checkpoints are not inherently more expressive than per-call control.

One agenda, phased evidence: measure model continuation value and timing → vary model/context → test integrated versus composed single-agent control. The three phases make the experiments tractable; each can yield a useful mechanism, dataset, or negative result without requiring the final integrated controller to win.
Positioning boundary

This is a provisional, setting-specific gap—not a claim that prior art lacks long-horizon or session-aware routing. The contribution must be evidence of net allocation value, not merely adding more decisions to one controller.

06

Mathematics

One episode objective. A phased action space.

The same argument as the research gap: execution evidence (H, r) → a feasible allocation (a) → whole-episode outcomes (Y, C, L). The primary objective is cheaper execution with preserved completion; checkpoint value estimates help choose actions, but do not certify that objective.

Primary episode objective
minπ Eπ[C]
subject toEπ[Y] ≥ pref − εEπ[L] ≤ BL

In words: minimize average total deployment cost while retaining the reference policy’s task-completion rate within a predeclared margin ε and staying within an average latency budget. This is the operating point in Sections 4 and 8, not a separate success-maximization objective.

Count prefix execution, routing, handoffs, lost cache, and failed attempts. Declare the reference policy, margin, and latency budget before test evaluation. These population constraints are distinct from runtime-enforced per-episode spend/deadline caps. If no policy meets them, report infeasibility rather than relaxing the target after seeing test results.

Checkpoint allocation
gate(Ht, rt) ∈ { hold, revise }
at ∈ F(Ht, rt)
at = (mt, κt, bt)

In words: hold preserves the current allocation until the next decision; revise selects an admissible alternative from F. The agent’s model, evidence packet, and generation/reasoning budget are bounded by the experiment.

F requires supported models, packets that fit the selected model and pool limits, and dispatch reservations/runtime resource controls. It does not guarantee that an action completes the task or meets the population success target.

Phase 1 fixes the budget and uses five fixed continuations; Phase 2 varies model/context; Phase 3 combines validated choices. The tuple is the broad agenda, not an instruction to learn every choice at once.

Read the symbols

Subscript t indexes a decision checkpoint and a a candidate continuation. Superscript “rem” means resources used after the checkpoint; a prime means the next checkpoint. E[·] denotes an expectation, and a hat (ˆ) denotes a learned estimate.

Policy and episode outcomes

π, πref
π is the routing policy mapping visible history/resources to allocation choices. πref is a predeclared reference policy, such as always Strong, under the same task population and scaffold.
E[·], Eπ[·]
An average over tasks and execution randomness. Eπ explicitly uses policy π. A checkpoint expectation conditions on its observed history/resources and the stated future protocol.
Y
Terminal success: 1 if the final task passes the declared checks, otherwise 0. E[Y] is the task-completion probability.
C, L
Total episode deployment cost C in a declared unit, such as dollars; end-to-end wall time L in seconds.
pref, ε
pref = Eπref[Y] is the reference completion probability. ε is the allowed absolute loss: 0.02 means two percentage points, not a 2% relative loss.
BL
The allowed population-average episode wall time. It is not a token budget or the individual episode deadline.

Observed state and chosen allocation

t; Ht, rt
t is a decision index, not elapsed seconds. Ht is the visible session history and current workspace evidence. rt is a vector of remaining shared resources: spend, deadline time, and tool/turn allowances.
xt, φ
xt = φ(Ht, rt) is the router’s feature vector. φ is the feature extractor, such as diagnostics plus a frozen history embedding.
at
at is an allocation from the phase’s allowed menu, including keeping the current configuration.
F(H, r)
The phase-allowed feasible menu given visible state and remaining resources. It checks configuration compatibility and resource controls; admissibility is not evidence of task success.
mt, κt
mt selects the agent’s model from the allowed pool. κt selects the evidence-packet policy, such as full history or a compact handoff—not the model’s native context-window capacity.
bt
The allowed generation/reasoning budget for the agent, using supported backend controls. It is distinct from context capacity and the shared remaining spend rt.

Continuation value and timing

Qa, μa
In Phase 1a, Qa is expected remaining weighted utility for branch a; μa fixes its continuation, such as Strong-full to termination without further routing.
Qπ
In later phases, value after allocation a with future allocation decisions following frozen policy π. Changing π changes the value.
Ya, Crema, Lrema
The resulting terminal success, additional cost, and additional wall time for branch a. Cost/time include its handoff and other downstream overhead, but exclude the already-spent prefix.
λ, η
Non-negative weights for the local utility surrogate: penalty per dollar and per second. Tune on development data. They are neither budgets nor the non-inferiority margin ε, and do not enforce the episode constraints.
Δa, Δπa
Phase 1a compares a with Cheap-to-end. The later-phase Δπa compares revising with holding under the same future policy. Positive means better weighted utility, not necessarily lower cost.
V, Vhold
Phase 1b’s best remaining weighted utility before its one intervention, and the value of continuing Cheap to the next decision with the option to intervene later. Neither is a cost-constraint certificate.
H′, r′
The next visible history and remaining-resource vector after executing the Cheap segment. They depend on its observations, spend, and elapsed time.
csegment, ℓsegment
Additional cost and wall time between this decision and the next. Lowercase c/ℓ are one segment; uppercase C/L are episode totals.
What is observable: task, visible tool history, current diff, progress/failure features, context occupancy, cache warmth, tool-loop/provider-state status, and remaining resources. Features xt = φ(Ht, rt) are a summary; they need not preserve everything required to predict the future. Hidden tests, future branches, and final outcomes are training/evaluation labels—not online inputs.

Phase 1a: estimate a fixed continuation’s value

Qa(Ht, rt; μa) = E[Ya − λCrema − ηLrema | Ht, rt; μa]
Δa = Qa − Qcheap-to-end

Read the conditional: “given the same observed checkpoint and remaining resources, and following the stated continuation protocol.” Compare continue-Cheap, Strong with full/compact context, and Strong restart. Fully sampled branches support supervised predictors; deployment with feedback only for the selected action is a contextual-bandit setting.

Q̂a = p̂a − λĈrema − ηL̂rema

What we train: p̂a estimates P(Ya = 1 | Ht, rt; μa); Ĉ and L̂ estimate remaining cost and time. These estimates come from executed training branches, not an oracle. Use Q̂ as a local ranking surrogate, not the primary cost objective. Tune λ/η on development data; penalized utility does not automatically satisfy the episode constraints. Accept operating points only after the declared completion-margin and latency checks on fresh, whole-policy episodes. The population success threshold is not a per-checkpoint cutoff on p̂a.

Worked example · fictional predictions

At one checkpoint, suppose both fixed continuations are feasible. Let λ = 0.20 per dollar and η = 0.001 per second; the remaining cost/time estimates include handoff overhead.

Continue Cheap

Predicted success 0.55 · $0.10 more · 40 s more

Q̂ = 0.55 − 0.20 × 0.10 − 0.001 × 40 = 0.49

Strong + full history

Predicted success 0.85 · $0.50 more · 60 s more

Q̂ = 0.85 − 0.20 × 0.50 − 0.001 × 60 = 0.69

Δ̂Strong = 0.69 − 0.49 = +0.20: Strong ranks higher under these weights. This is not a cost saving or proof of population-level non-inferiority, and does not yet show switching now is better than waiting. If the prefix already used $0.20 and 30 s, Strong’s predicted whole-episode totals are $0.70 and 90 s; sunk prefix resources cancel in this local comparison but remain in the reported totals.

Phase 1b: value of waiting before one intervention

V(H, r) = max{ Vhold(H, r), maxa ∈ feasible interventions Qa(H, r) }
Vhold(H, r) = E[−λcsegment − ηℓsegment + V(H′, r′) | H, r, hold]

In words: compare intervening now with paying for another Cheap segment and deciding again on the new evidence. Terminal V equals Y, and recursion stops when the episode terminates. This recursion concerns weighted utility, not a direct solution of the constrained cost objective. “Cheap to the end” commits to never intervening; Vhold preserves the future option. They are not the same quantity.

Estimate that future option from additional transitions/branches or fitted policy evaluation. The equations define values; they do not make them directly observable. Restrict actions using the documented spend/deadline controls—predicted means alone cannot guarantee hard caps. Freeze the resulting policy before fresh closed-loop evaluation.

Phases 2–3: extend the same value to context and repeated decisions

Qπ(H, r, a) = E[Ya − λCrema − ηLrema | H, r, a; π]
Δπa = Qπ(H, r, a) − Qπ(H, r, hold)

In words: execute the candidate allocation a from the same restored checkpoint, then follow the named future controller π. Here a can change the model/context pair (and, in Phase 3, the reasoning budget) as its phase permits. Holding retains the current allocation until the next decision, followed by the same π—not a commitment to Cheap-to-end.

μa fixes a complete Phase 1a branch; π may make later decisions in these phases. Freeze that continuation policy when collecting or comparing labels. Offline values from one policy do not automatically transfer to another.

Repeated revisions and variable decision intervals fit a history/belief-based constrained semi-Markov framework. Remaining latency is the resulting elapsed wall time. Cache loss, context transfer, and re-reading costs belong in the measured continuation.

The integrated policy must improve episode cost under the same completion/latency conditions as the composed baseline; a higher surrogate Q alone is insufficient. This framework supports the broad agenda, not solving the full action space in one training run. Deliberation-cost options and Cost of Commitment provide relevant foundations.
