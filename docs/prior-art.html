03

Prior art

One literature map, organized by the decision.

Read each family in the same way: what the work establishes, then how it informs our experiment. Request routers, long-horizon controllers, serving frameworks, and benchmarks are different kinds of prior art—not competing names for the same thing.

A · Request and policy routing

Which model should receive this request?

Baselines · all phases

Avengers-Pro

Learned router · v2

Established contributionQuery embeddings, clusters, and model performance–efficiency scores support single-pass model selection.

Role in our studyCompare original-request, retrained per-call, and retrained checkpoint-state variants. Match tasks, pool, scaffold, and total budget; isolate gains from representation, labels, or extra data from gains attributed to decision timing.

LongContextRouter

Task-level router for agents · ICLR 2027 submission · §§3–5

Established contributionPicks one model per agent task before execution from a hierarchical encoding of long task inputs (overlapping windows + causal Transformer), with a success head and a separate cost head (Um = π̂m − λφ(ĉm)). On SWE-bench Pro, history text is used only in training; at test it sees the problem statement only. Five-model pool, four benchmarks, one split.

Role in our studyClosest request-level baseline for agent tasks, and evidence that execution history helps a router. It never decides mid-run and never chooses what context the agent receives, so it does not cover D3/D4. Its future work asks for “step-level routing” with “richer decision-time histories”, which is CPR’s setting.

RouteLLM · PILOT

Preference / bandit routers

Established contributionRouteLLM learns a strong/weak preference trade-off; PILOT learns from contextual-bandit feedback. Their original decision unit is an incoming request.

Role in our studyGround economical model selection and chosen-action feedback. Reusing these ideas at checkpoints requires state and outcome supervision appropriate to the remaining episode; it is an adaptation, not proof that request routers cannot be extended.

vLLM Semantic Router

Deployment framework · official architecture

Established contributionConversation/session signals, projections, policy, memory/plugins, and bounded multi-model algorithms. Session-aware routing adds tool-loop and provider-state hard locks, idle/drift resets, prefix-cache checkout cost, switch-history and handoff penalties, and replay traces. It is not merely a task classifier. The agent harness owns the task loop, tool execution, and durable workspace state.

Role in our studyFreeze a version and explicit signal/policy configuration; match the pool and admissible evidence. Capture checkpoints in the harness. Do not assume conversation memory is absent, or conflate logical model selection with healthy-replica scheduling. Reuse its continuity locks and cache-aware switch cost as infrastructure and as a session-aware baseline.

B · Sequential model control

When should execution stay, defer, or switch?

Phase 1 · evidence + timing

MTRouter

Per-turn routing · §3.3 · v1

Established contributionHistory and candidate-model representations drive turn-level routing; terminal outcomes and future-error penalties provide supervision. ScienceWorld and HLE evaluation.

Role in our studyTerminal supervision is not our novelty. Compare checkpoint and per-call timing with matched state/outcome predictors and a stay action; only then isolate later context choices.

ReDAct · TRIM

Local deferral / reasoning-step control

Established contributionReDAct defers uncertain local decisions in ALFWorld/MiniGrid. TRIM uses reasoning-step correctness and uncertainty to allocate stronger-model effort.

Role in our studyCompare local risk/uncertainty intervention with remaining-episode value. Distinguish temporary deferral followed by Cheap from reversible model switching; disclose faithful reproductions versus logit-free analogues.

EvoRoute

Feedback-driven workflow routing · v1

Established contributionExperience and execution feedback inform quality–cost–latency routing.

Role in our studyStateful feedback is not new. Test whether executed alternative continuations add useful supervision beyond cheaper progress/risk signals under matched data and capacity.

C · Context and handoff

What evidence should the selected model receive?

Phase 2 · model × context

Routed Graph Handoff

Handoff representation

Established contributionSelects structured dependency-graph versus natural-language handoffs.

Role in our studyContext transfer is prior art. Compare predefined full/compact packets and their interaction with model selection, charging construction, re-reading, and omitted-evidence effects.

FleetOpt

Serving-pool capacity · v2

Established contributionStudies effective-context-window serving pools through calibrated analytical simulation.

Role in our studySeparate native model capacity, serving-pool limits, and the evidence actually sent. Our task-level context-allocation experiment needs executable terminal outcomes; a serving simulation is not that evidence.

E · Continuation data and evaluation

How are alternative routes given outcome labels?

Data + evaluation · all phases

TwinRouterBench

Branching benchmark · §§3–5 · v4

Established contributionBranches intermediate prefixes and verifies downstream success. Static construction starts from successful Strong traces and tests tier replacements with the same step count; live evaluation includes prompt caching.

Role in our studyBranching is not new. Sample ordinary and degraded Cheap-created states with repeated outcomes; later add bounded context alternatives. Compare checkpoint coverage, continuation protocol, and real cache/resource accounting.

F · Foundations and orthogonal background

Useful tools—not the proposed novelty.

Conceptual grounding

AutoMix · Router-R1 · RLCascadeRouter · TREACLE

Sequential-control foundations

Established contributionProvide cascade, POMDP, and reinforcement-learning foundations for sequential cost-aware routing.

Role in our studyUse established decision-process tools. The experiment concerns checkpoint resource allocation in persistent tool environments, not the invention of sequential control.

LLM Router · Sqwish report

Representation paper / engineering blog

Established contributionLLM Router predicts target-model correctness from prefill representations. Sqwish describes model–budget outcome prediction in an engineering report.

Role in our studyKeep representation and budget prediction as background. The proposal requires neither prefill access nor a historical leaderboard claim; the blog is not treated as equivalent evidence to a research paper.

What this leaves open

Adaptive handoff, terminal-outcome learning, session continuity, and context transfer already exist. Our agenda is checkpoint-timed allocation of model, context, and budget within a single agent session. Its contribution must be measured allocation effects and interactions that improve on strong component and composed-policy baselines. The research gap turns these overlaps into specific, falsifiable questions.
