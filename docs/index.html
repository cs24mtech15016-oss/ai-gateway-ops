Research proposal · not an experimental result

Can this task still be rescued?

A stronger model may solve a task from scratch yet fail to recover the state a cheaper agent has already created. CheckpointRouter studies that conditional recovery value: when to continue, transfer context, or restart—within the episode’s remaining budget.

01

Premise

Difficulty—and recoverability—change during execution.

A software agent does not just generate answers. It changes files, executes tools, and accumulates a history that determines what is possible next.

Repository inspection and test feedback can reveal information missing from the opening request. But evidence of difficulty is not evidence that switching will help. A cheap agent might be close to success; a stronger agent might inherit misleading edits; or a restart might consume the remaining deadline.

Adaptive handoff is already studied by TACIT-Switch. Our candidate question concerns measured continuation outcomes from the actual checkpoint, rather than treating initial strong-model success as proof of rescue.

02

Problem

Predict the benefit of intervening—not just the risk of failure.

Timing

Keep working, or intervene now?

Use only evidence available at the checkpoint. Continuing may preserve the option to intervene later; stopping consumes the one permitted intervention.

gate(history, budget) ∈ { hold, intervene }
Continuation

Transfer, compress, or restart?

Estimate the terminal benefit and remaining cost of each feasible continuation. After an intervention, the strong model completes the episode in the first study.

intervene ∈ { full, compact, restart }
Request-levelWhich model initially?

A useful baseline, without future execution evidence at decision time.

Per-callWhich model next?

Can use history and terminal supervision. A stay action can also emulate sparse switching.

Recovery studyWhich intervention helps here?

Compare actual checkpoint continuations, including no intervention and clean restart.

Focused research question

How can a controller estimate the incremental benefit of model intervention from a partially completed software-agent task, and choose between continuing, transferring with full or compact context, and restarting under remaining cost and latency budgets?

The first study measures and predicts recovery under fixed continuation protocols. Adaptive timing follows only if that signal is useful. Context-pool allocation and bounded sub-agents are extensions—not an unproven claim to invent joint orchestration.

Positioning boundary

The opportunity is a provisional, testable distinction in checkpoint-specific recovery supervision and intervention choice. It is not “the first long-horizon router,” “the first terminal-outcome router,” “the first adaptive handoff,” or “the first joint multi-agent controller.”

04

The defensible gap

Initial competence is not checkpoint recoverability.

The candidate contribution is evidence about when intervention really helps from a Cheap-created state, which handoff preserves that benefit, and whether the benefit is predictable before the outcome is known.

Gap statement: a difference in the estimand and experimental protocol, not a claim that prior art is absent

Primary, falsifiable hypothesis

At a matched checkpoint and budget, a calibrated action-conditioned recovery predictor will choose interventions with better held-out terminal success–cost trade-offs than task-only, risk-only, or progress-only predictors. Full/compact/restart choices must each earn their additional complexity.

What would falsify the opportunity?

If initial Strong success or a simple repeated-failure rule predicts recovery just as well—or all handoff variants behave alike—then direct branch collection and a learned intervention selector may not be worth building.

Novelty remains provisional

This is a scoped synthesis and empirical hypothesis, not proof of an untouched literature gap. A broader prior-art search and direct reproductions may show that the useful contribution is a recovery dataset or an evaluation result rather than a new routing algorithm.

Mechanisms to investigate

Why a request router may need different training—not why all per-call routers fail.

A history-aware, outcome-trained per-call policy can represent many checkpoint policies. Sparse decisions do not have greater expressive power by themselves. Any advantage must come from learning efficiency, measured overhead, or better treatment of state and handoffs.

01 · Missing evidence

The opening request is incomplete.

Test whether repository and tool evidence add predictive value beyond the task description. SWE-Router motivates exploratory information; its idealized analysis does not guarantee net savings for a finite learned router.

02 · Wrong target

Risk is not intervention benefit.

A visible failure may be easy for Cheap to fix, or impossible for Strong to recover. Predict each continuation’s outcome, not just the next response’s correctness. MTRouter already uses terminal supervision.

03 · Policy shift

Routing changes future states.

Cheap, Strong, and mixed policies visit different workspaces and histories. Offline checkpoint results need a fresh closed-loop test; they are not automatically valid for every induced trajectory.

04 · Handoff information

Compression can remove useful evidence.

Measure loss of failed attempts, constraints, file references, and provenance in textual handoffs. Do not assume an invisible, persistent internal “plan” is lost between ordinary stateless model calls.

05 · Real resources

Switching need not be free—or frequent.

Model changes may require cache re-computation, depending on provider and cache policy. A per-call controller can repeatedly stay. Compare actual cache hits, encoder/gate time, and transfer cost.

06 · Delayed outcomes

Good-looking progress can mislead.

Terminal execution tests can disagree with local quality or progress scores. Restoring a common snapshot and executing alternative continuations estimates a local intervention effect under a specified protocol.

05

Mathematics

Start with conditional outcomes. Add stopping only when justified.

Episode objective
maxπ E[Y]
subject toE[C] ≤ BCE[L] ≤ BL

Y is terminal task success, C total deployment cost, and L total episode wall time. These are population-average constraints, not guarantees on each episode. Separate harness-enforced per-episode spend and deadline limits define which continuations are admissible.

Fixed continuation value
Qa(Ht, rt) = E[Ya − λCrema − ηLrema | Ht, rt; μa]

Ht is observable history; rt is remaining budget. μa fixes the post-action scaffold and model policy. λ and η price cost and time; sweep them on development data.

Δa = Qa − Qcheap-to-end

This is the incremental value of committing now versus completing with Cheap. Remaining cost includes handoff preparation and re-reading. Common sunk prefix cost cancels in this local comparison but remains in episode accounting.

Observable input: task, visible tool history, current diff, failure/repetition/progress features, context occupancy, and remaining resources. The restored sandbox is available to the executor; the router sees only permitted pre-decision observations. A compact feature vector x = φ(H) need not be Markov-sufficient. Hidden tests, future branches, and final outcomes are labels—not inputs.

1. One checkpoint: contextual action selection

Sample a checkpoint, compare four fixed continuations, and fit success/cost/time predictors with supervised losses. Fully sampled branches provide local full-feedback data. If deployment observes only the chosen action, this becomes contextual-bandit feedback; no large RL run is required for the first study.

2. Several checkpoints: optimal stopping

V(H, r) = max{ Vhold(H, r), maxa ∈ interventions Qa(H, r) }
Vhold = E[−λcsegment − ηℓsegment + V(H′, r′)]

Holding preserves a later intervention. At a terminal state, V equals the terminal reward. “Cheap to the end” is not Vhold. Estimate future-option value from additional Cheap-prefix transitions and intervention branches, or fitted policy evaluation; validate on fresh episodes rather than using future outcomes as online features.

3. Repeated interventions: a later control problem

With returns to Cheap, delegation, or variable commitment duration, use a history/belief-based constrained semi-Markov decision process. Duration, cache state, and coordination costs belong in transitions and rewards. Deliberation-cost options and Cost of Commitment are relevant foundations—not a novelty claim.

A bounded experiment before a general controller.

One software agent, one Cheap/Strong pair, one immutable scaffold, and at most one intervention. Model labels describe measured capability/cost in this setting, not universal rankings.

A · Control

Continue Cheap

Keep the same workspace and full allowable history. Cheap runs to termination; no later escalation in the single-checkpoint experiment.

B · In-place recovery

Strong + full history

Restore the checkpoint. Strong receives the full permitted history and completes the task without further routing.

C · Context intervention

Strong + compact handoff

Use the same workspace, but a fixed, token-capped packet: goal, current diff, visible failures, constraints, attempted fixes, and evidence references. Charge packet construction and verification.

D · Clean restart

Strong + initial task

Restore the original sandbox, not the edited checkpoint. Give the original task only. Prefix spend and elapsed time remain sunk; restart receives the residual budget, not a fresh episode.

What a snapshot must preserve

Base commit and workspace changes; container/dependency state; working directory and relevant shell/tool state; tool outputs and agent messages; random/configuration metadata; elapsed time and remaining spend/turn/tool limits. Snapshot only at quiescent tool boundaries. Test replay equivalence before trusting comparisons.

If a process or external service cannot be cloned consistently, restrict or document it. Do not silently call a message transcript a restorable environment.

Sampling without hindsight leakage

Include early, middle, and late checkpoints; stalled/error states; and normal low-risk/successful controls. Sample using visible prefix evidence. If final outcomes stratify analysis or sampling, keep them out of model inputs and report sampling weights and the resulting coverage.

All sibling checkpoints/branches stay in their task’s split. Group related issues and near-duplicates; hold out repositories where possible. Branch labels come from hidden terminal tests unavailable to the online router.

Comparability and uncertainty

Fix system prompts, tools, model versions, token limits, and stopping rules. Use a common residual spend/deadline and documented residual turn/tool caps. Do not force equal completion lengths; extra recovery steps are charged. Reserve worst-case call cost before dispatch and enforce caps.

Repeat stochastic continuations with recorded seeds where supported. Store each trial, not just the best outcome; use uncertainty intervals and test calibration. Differences identify effects in this controlled snapshot population—not general causal effects on all tasks.

Learn the simplest useful model

Start with tabular diagnostics plus a frozen history embedding, then logistic/gradient-boosted outcome and resource predictors. Fit per-action success likelihoods and cost/time regressions; select feasible actions using calibrated utility. Compare task-only and risk/progress proxies with equal capacity and training volume.

Feature extraction, gate inference, and serialization are deployment costs. No prefill activation access, new foundation-model training, or unconstrained agent graph is required.

Phase 1 · Primary study

Measure and predict rescue

Use one sampled checkpoint per episode for online action selection. Establish recovery variation, packet/restart differences, held-out calibration, and improvement over inexpensive proxies.

Phase 2 · Conditional next step

Learn when to stop

Only if Phase 1 has useful signal: collect multi-checkpoint Cheap continuations, account for the option to intervene later, and compare fixed-prefix, per-call, and event-gated timing under the same action/value model.

Phase 3 · Extensions

Expand the action space carefully

Study multiple model/context configurations or one bounded worker at a time. Admit more complex orchestration only when it improves terminal outcomes after coordination overhead.

08

Evaluation

Test the claim—not a weaker opponent.

Offline action prediction is a diagnostic. The decisive result is a fresh, closed-loop episode comparison with all policy overhead included.

Required comparison families

  1. Always Cheap / always Strong: initial-state endpoints under identical scaffold and episode caps.
  2. Task-only and simple rules: initial Strong-success/difficulty predictors, repeated-failure escalation, and fixed role/model tables where applicable.
  3. Avengers-Pro: original-request, retrained per-call, and retrained checkpoint-state variants.
  4. vLLM Semantic Router: a versioned, explicitly configured signal-and-policy baseline—not an unspecified product label.
  5. SWE-Router-like fixed prefix: development-selected prefix length and continue/restart threshold.
  6. TACIT-Switch-style handoff: paired-rollout/teacher-interval supervision under matched tasks, with annotation cost disclosed.
  7. ReDAct-style deferral: local uncertainty/risk intervention followed by Cheap; distinguish faithful reproduction from a logit-free analogue.
  8. MTRouter-like outcome-aware per-call: history and terminal labels, with a stay action. Equalize available state, model pool, and training budget.

What to measure

Terminal successExecutable task checks; no LLM judge as the only correctness criterion.
Total episode spendPrefix, failed attempts, handoff generation, cache reads/writes, routing, and tools.
Wall time + tailsEnd-to-end latency, p50/p95, transfer time, tool waits, and cap violations.
Conditional recoveryRescue/no-rescue distributions across pre-decision failure, stage, and budget strata.
Prediction qualityPer-action calibration, Brier/log loss, ranking error, and uncertainty—not accuracy alone.
Decision overheadEncoder, gate, value prediction, serialization, and cache re-computation separately.
Intervention behaviorTiming, unnecessary/late switches, context size, and restart frequency.
GeneralizationHeld-out repositories/tasks, policy-induced states, and a later model-pair transfer test.
Primary operating pointMinimize total cost subject to a predeclared non-inferiority margin on terminal success, with an explicit latency budget.

Matched ablations

Hold the state encoder and action-value model fixed when comparing fixed-prefix, every-call, and event-gated timing. Separately compare task-only, risk/progress proxies, and measured branch targets; then full/compact/restart packets. Report data volume, annotation effort, and adaptation differences rather than attributing every gain to the gate.

Statistical discipline

Choose prices, budgets, margins, thresholds, and primary outcomes before test evaluation. Use repeated runs and task/repository-clustered paired confidence intervals. Report absolute success-rate loss and cost reduction, failures, and all operating points; tune only on development data.

If reporting “retain 95% or 98% of Strong’s success,” define it as a relative success-rate target, not 95% task accuracy. Do not promise exact equality or superiority in advance.

09

Oracle and cost

Hindsight is a diagnostic—not a deployable policy.

A request-level “perfect oracle” uses known candidate outcomes. Here the diagnostic chooses among only the sampled checkpoint actions under their stated continuation protocols.

Define the local hindsight selector

a*i = argmaxa ∈ Ai [p̂selectia − λĉselectia − ηℓ̂selectia]

Ai contains sampled feasible branches. Estimate success probability and mean resource use with repeated continuations; specify tie-breaking. Select with one repetition set and report with independent repetitions to reduce winner’s-curse bias.

Report every checkpoint, including cases where no branch succeeds. One lucky success among many rollouts is not the model’s expected recovery probability.

Keep three numbers separate

Deployment route cost: sunk prefix + selected remaining branch cost (including its transfer) + router overhead not already included. Never charge handoff twice.

Data-collection cost: actual prefix runs + all branch continuations and repetitions + snapshots, infrastructure, annotations, and evaluation. Do not sum the prefix again for each clone.

Diagnostic performance: independent outcome estimates for the selected sampled branch. A budget-constrained selector and an unconstrained success oracle are different diagnostics.

Not a global upper bound

This selector sees hindsight estimates only for the collected states and actions. It does not optimize every possible future policy, graph, model, or context packet; finite noisy estimates may even underperform another policy. Call it a sampled-branch hindsight diagnostic, not a proven full-episode oracle ceiling.

10

Feasibility

Practical to pilot. Not yet justified to scale.

A credible first experiment

Small action space

  • One agent, two measured model endpoints.
  • One intervention with three Strong continuations.
  • Quiescent, reproducible software sandboxes.
  • Small supervised predictors before complex RL.
  • A pilot sized to measure effect and noise before committing a larger data budget.
Not yet a credible promise

Unbounded control

  • Arbitrary agent graphs and model pools.
  • Exhaustive branching across a whole episode.
  • Guaranteed frontier success at lower cost.
  • Assumed millisecond end-to-end router latency.
  • A fixed dollar budget derived from an unmeasured per-rollout price.
Measure before budgeting

Collection bill

prefix runs
+ Σ continuation trials
+ infrastructure
+ annotation / evaluation

Use pilot token/cache usage and runtimes for mean and tail estimates. Prices, model versions, repeated trials, failed branches, storage, and snapshot restoration must appear in the estimate.

Go / no-go decision

Proceed only if checkpoints exhibit meaningful intervention-value differences, the predictor generalizes, and net episode savings survive routing and transfer overhead. If a simple rule matches it, prefer the rule. A negative recovery result or a well-audited dataset can still be a worthwhile research contribution; deployment benefit is not assumed.

11

Extensions: context pools and sub-agents

Expand what an intervention can do—one factor at a time.

These are motivated follow-up questions, not prerequisites for the first study or claims of novelty over MasRouter, ProgRouter, CASTER, or routed handoffs.

01

Model × context configuration

A long-native-context model, a cheaper short-context model with a compact packet, and the same model with more history can yield different recovery values. Hold task/scaffold fixed and separate model capability from packet length and content.

02

Preserve evidence, not just tokens

Compare raw history, structured evidence packets, retrieval, and graph handoffs. Measure omitted constraints and provenance, packet creation cost, and terminal recovery—not only compression ratio.

03

One bounded worker

At a qualifying checkpoint, compare coordinator-only recovery against a read-only diagnosis or test-analysis worker. Fix the role and cap its calls; test Cheap versus Strong assignment with a predefined merge protocol.

04

Per-worker model assignment

Given a fixed role, local evidence packet, dependencies, and shared remaining budget, estimate that worker’s incremental contribution to terminal success. Progress-guided worker routing already exists; isolate recovery target and handoff effects.

05

Limited parallelism

Only after a useful one-worker result, permit at most two workers. Measure duplicate effort, merge conflicts, shared-budget contention, and critical-path latency. Parallel wall time is not the sum of worker runtimes; billed cost generally is additive.

06

Serving-aware constraints

Record actual input/output/cache tokens, queue time, and effective context limits. For self-hosted pools, benchmark KV memory and concurrency on the target stack rather than treating a simulation’s throughput assumptions as universal.

Three different notions: a model’s native maximum context, the actual evidence packet supplied to it, and a serving pool’s configured/effective window. A larger allowed window is not automatically a larger API bill; actual token/cache usage and provider rules determine it. FleetOpt’s analytical pool results and Lost in the Middle motivate measurements, not guaranteed savings or universal claims about modern models.

Proposed contributions

Evidence first. Algorithmic novelty only if earned.

01

A checkpoint recovery dataset with reproducible workspace restoration, action definitions, repeated outcomes, and resource accounting.

02

An empirical test of whether initial competence, risk, and progress proxies predict real conditional rescue—and where they fail.

03

A calibrated, budget-aware intervention selector with fair single-pass, per-call, fixed-prefix, and adaptive-handoff baselines.

04

A measured handoff/restart trade-off and explicit criteria for whether timing, larger context pools, or bounded workers merit further work.

Status: research formulation. No checkpoint controller, recovery dataset, or claimed success–cost result has been implemented in this repository.
