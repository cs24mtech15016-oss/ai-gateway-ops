Research proposal · long-horizon agents

Route at the moments that actually matter.

CheckpointRouter studies when a long-running system should change its execution plan—and which model, context configuration, and reasoning budget should power the coordinator or each spawned sub-agent. Decisions use execution evidence and optimize the whole episode.

01

Premise

Difficulty changes while an agent works.

A software-engineering agent moves through exploration, planning, editing, testing, diagnosis, recovery, and verification. These phases may demand different model capabilities, context, and coordination.

Initial prompts are incomplete routing signals: important evidence often appears only after repository inspection, tool execution, or worker feedback. The opportunity is to allocate resources as that evidence arrives, rather than treating the opening request as a complete description of the episode.

Routing does not have to mean escalating after failure. It can also mean changing a context packet, assigning a model to a new worker, reducing effort during verification, or deliberately staying with the current plan. Whether these choices help must be measured at the episode level, including their overhead.

02

Problem

A coupled decision with a delayed outcome.

Decision A · Timing

When should the system route?

Decide whether new trajectory evidence makes reconsideration valuable enough to justify routing, handoff, or coordination overhead. Keeping the current allocation is a valid action.

open_checkpoint(history, budget) ∈ { no, yes }
Decision B · Allocation

What execution team should continue?

Continue centrally, switch the coordinator, or spawn a bounded sub-agent; then assign each active role a model, context configuration, and reasoning budget. Introduce these choices in separate experimental phases.

action = graph × model × context × budget
Request-levelWhich model initially?

A useful baseline, without future execution evidence at decision time.

Per-callWhich model next?

Can use history and terminal supervision. A stay action can also emulate sparse switching.

CheckpointWhen and how should the plan change?

Use information-bearing events to reconsider the allocation and judge its remaining-episode value.

Research statement

How can a long-horizon system determine when to revise its execution graph and which model–context configuration should power each continuing or newly spawned agent, given shared state, local histories, progress, failures, dependencies, and budget?

The objective is to preserve frontier-model task completion while reducing total episode cost and latency—including coordination and context-transfer overhead—and avoiding routing or delegation decisions that add no value. This is the overarching research agenda; the phases below bound each experiment without replacing that agenda with failure recovery alone.

Positioning boundary

The research agenda is checkpoint-timed allocation of model, context, budget, and bounded sub-agent resources, evaluated by terminal episode outcomes. Adaptive handoff, terminal-outcome learning, and joint orchestration already exist. The contribution must come from measured allocation effects, interactions, and a controller that improves on strong component and composed-policy baselines—not from claiming those capabilities are new.

04

Research gap

The episode couples timing, context, and agent assignments.

The question is not whether any of these choices exists. It is when evidence-driven, coordinated resource allocation is worth learning instead of composing existing routers, fixed context policies, and role/model tables.

Provisional gap: isolate each decision, then test whether its interaction with the others matters

Overarching, falsifiable hypothesis

Execution evidence can support selective model–context assignments and bounded delegation that improve the episode success–cost–latency frontier over task-only, per-call, fixed-role, and composed-policy baselines. Each phase tests one part; the integrated phase must show value beyond its independently tuned components.

Clear falsifier

If a per-call policy with a stay action, fixed model/context/role table, or simple composition matches the learned controller after all overhead is counted, the corresponding learned allocation is not justified. A joint controller must earn its complexity; more decision axes alone are not a contribution.

Broad vision, specific evidence

Recovery is one informative checkpoint case, not the whole research problem. The gap remains provisional: the phased studies may contribute a dataset, an allocation mechanism, a negative result, or evidence that simple policies suffice. They do not establish that prior art lacks long-horizon or joint routing.

Mechanisms to investigate

Why a request router may need different training—not why all per-call routers fail.

A history-aware, outcome-trained per-call policy can represent many checkpoint policies. Sparse decisions do not have greater expressive power by themselves. Any advantage must come from learning efficiency, measured overhead, or better treatment of state and handoffs.

01 · Missing evidence

The opening request is incomplete.

Test whether repository and tool evidence add predictive value beyond the task description. SWE-Router motivates exploratory information; its idealized analysis does not guarantee net savings for a finite learned router.

02 · Wrong target

Risk is not intervention benefit.

A visible failure may be easy for Cheap to fix, or impossible for Strong to recover. Predict each continuation’s outcome, not just the next response’s correctness. MTRouter already uses terminal supervision.

03 · Policy shift

Routing changes future states.

Cheap, Strong, and mixed policies visit different workspaces and histories. Offline checkpoint results need a fresh closed-loop test; they are not automatically valid for every induced trajectory.

04 · Handoff information

Compression can remove useful evidence.

Measure loss of failed attempts, constraints, file references, and provenance in textual handoffs. Do not assume an invisible, persistent internal “plan” is lost between ordinary stateless model calls.

05 · Real resources

Switching need not be free—or frequent.

Model changes may require cache re-computation, depending on provider and cache policy. A per-call controller can repeatedly stay. Compare actual cache hits, encoder/gate time, and transfer cost.

06 · Delayed outcomes

Good-looking progress can mislead.

Terminal execution tests can disagree with local quality or progress scores. Restoring a common snapshot and executing alternative continuations estimates a local intervention effect under a specified protocol.

05

Mathematics

One episode objective. A phased action space.

Episode objective
maxπ E[Y]
subject toE[C] ≤ BCE[L] ≤ BL

Y is terminal task success, C total deployment cost across the coordinator and workers, and L end-to-end wall time. Include routing, handoff, coordination, and failed attempts. Population-average constraints are separate from harness-enforced per-episode spend and deadline caps.

Checkpoint allocation
gate(Ht, rt) ∈ { hold, revise }
at = (gt, { (mi, κi, bi) }i ∈ A(gt))

g changes a bounded execution graph; A(g) contains active roles. Each role receives a model m, context configuration κ, and reasoning/token budget b. κ distinguishes the evidence packet from the model/pool’s context capacity. Holding preserves the current allocation.

H is observed global/local history and dependencies; r is remaining shared resources. Phase 1 fixes the graph and budget menu, Phase 2 varies model/context, Phase 3 introduces workers, and Phase 4 combines validated choices.

Observable input: task, visible tool history, current diff, progress/failure features, active roles and local histories, dependencies, context occupancy, and remaining resources. Router features x = φ(H) need not be Markov-sufficient. Hidden tests, future branches, and final outcomes are labels—not inputs.

General framework: constrained sequential control

For repeated revisions and variable commitment durations, use a history/belief-based constrained semi-Markov decision process. Specify the continuation policy when estimating action value; changing future routing or worker behavior changes that value. Duration, cache state, coordination, and merge effects belong in transitions and resource accounting. This is a modeling framework, not a claim to solve the full action space in one training run.

Deliberation-cost options and Cost of Commitment provide relevant foundations.

Phase 1a: fixed checkpoint continuations

Qa(Ht, rt; μa) = E[Ya − λCrema − ηLrema | Ht, rt; μa]
Δa = Qa − Qcheap-to-end

μ fixes post-action behavior. Compare continue-Cheap, Strong with full or compact context, and Strong restart. Fully sampled branches support supervised action-conditioned outcome/resource predictors; deployment with chosen-action feedback is a contextual-bandit setting. Recovery is one analysis stratum, alongside ordinary progress and no-intervention cases. Common sunk prefix cost cancels locally but remains in episode totals.

Phase 1b: adaptive timing with one intervention

V(H, r) = max{ Vhold(H, r), maxa ∈ interventions Qa(H, r) }
Vhold = E[−λcsegment − ηℓsegment + V(H′, r′)]

Holding preserves a later intervention. Terminal V equals the terminal reward. “Cheap to the end” is not Vhold; estimate the future option from additional transitions/branches or fitted policy evaluation. Later phases allow richer allocations, with their continuation protocols stated explicitly. Freeze decisions before evaluating fresh closed-loop episodes.

06

Method and phases

Keep the research vision. Bound each experiment.

The phases separate timing, model/context allocation, and sub-agent assignment before testing their interactions. They scope implementation and evidence—not the overarching research statement.

Phase 1 · Model control

Checkpoint evidence + timing

One agent, two models, fixed tools and budgets. First measure four continuations from a sampled checkpoint; then compare fixed-prefix, per-call, and adaptive one-way handoff using a matched value model.

Evidence: continuation value, recovery/no-intervention strata, timing overhead, and terminal success–cost curves.

Phase 2 · Context allocation

Model × context pool

A bounded two/three-model pool with different context capacities and two predefined full/compact packet policies. Keep graph and reasoning settings fixed; compare fixed, independently tuned, and joint model/context assignments.

Evidence: context sufficiency, actual token/cache use, and whether context choice changes the preferred model.

Phase 3 · Sub-agent assignment

Who works—and on which model?

Introduce one, then at most two workers with fixed diagnosis/test-analysis roles and a predefined merge protocol. Assign each a model/context pair under a shared budget; compare coordinator-only and fixed-role policies.

Evidence: terminal contribution, delegation frequency, duplicate work, merge cost, and critical-path latency.

Phase 4 · Integrated control

Test the coupled decisions

Combine validated timing, model/context, and bounded-worker choices. Compare a learned controller with composed component policies. Add a small reasoning-budget menu only in a separate ablation if justified.

Evidence: interaction gains beyond components, reliability under shared caps, and net deployment benefit.

First executable study: Phase 1a, not the entire proposal

One software agent, one Cheap/Strong pair, an immutable scaffold, and at most one intervention. Sample checkpoints across normal progress, planning/testing transitions, and stalled states. The four branches below make outcome collection tractable; model labels describe measured capability/cost in this setting, not universal rankings.

What a snapshot must preserve

Base commit and workspace changes; container/dependency state; working directory and relevant shell/tool state; tool outputs and agent messages; random/configuration metadata; elapsed time and remaining spend/turn/tool limits. Snapshot only at quiescent tool boundaries. Test replay equivalence before trusting comparisons.

If a process or external service cannot be cloned consistently, restrict or document it. Do not silently call a message transcript a restorable environment.

Sampling without hindsight leakage

Include early, middle, and late checkpoints; stalled/error states; and normal low-risk/successful controls. Sample using visible prefix evidence. If final outcomes stratify analysis or sampling, keep them out of model inputs and report sampling weights and the resulting coverage.

All sibling checkpoints/branches stay in their task’s split. Group related issues and near-duplicates; hold out repositories where possible. Branch labels come from hidden terminal tests unavailable to the online router.

Comparability and uncertainty

Fix system prompts, tools, model versions, token limits, and stopping rules. Use a common residual spend/deadline and documented residual turn/tool caps. Do not force equal completion lengths; extra recovery steps are charged. Reserve worst-case call cost before dispatch and enforce caps.

Repeat stochastic continuations with recorded seeds where supported. Store each trial, not just the best outcome; use uncertainty intervals and test calibration. Differences identify effects in this controlled snapshot population—not general causal effects on all tasks.

Learn the simplest useful model

Start with tabular diagnostics plus a frozen history embedding, then logistic/gradient-boosted outcome and resource predictors. Fit per-action success likelihoods and cost/time regressions; select feasible actions using calibrated utility. Compare task-only and risk/progress proxies with equal capacity and training volume.

Feature extraction, gate inference, and serialization are deployment costs. No prefill activation access, new foundation-model training, or unconstrained agent graph is required.

08

Evaluation

Test the claim—not a weaker opponent.

Each phase has a matched action space and baselines. Offline prediction is diagnostic; the decisive result is a fresh, closed-loop episode comparison including routing, context-transfer, worker, and merge overhead.

Required comparison families

  1. Always Cheap / always Strong: initial-state endpoints under identical scaffold and episode caps.
  2. Task-only and simple rules: initial Strong-success/difficulty predictors, repeated-failure escalation, and fixed role/model tables where applicable.
  3. Avengers-Pro: original-request, retrained per-call, and retrained checkpoint-state variants.
  4. vLLM Semantic Router: a versioned, explicitly configured signal-and-policy baseline—not an unspecified product label.
  5. SWE-Router-like fixed prefix: development-selected prefix length and continue/restart threshold.
  6. TACIT-Switch-style handoff: paired-rollout/teacher-interval supervision under matched tasks, with annotation cost disclosed.
  7. ReDAct-style deferral: local uncertainty/risk intervention followed by Cheap; distinguish faithful reproduction from a logit-free analogue.
  8. MTRouter-like outcome-aware per-call: history and terminal labels, with a stay action. Equalize available state, model pool, and training budget.
  9. Phase 2 context policies: always-full, fixed compact/retrieval packets, and independently tuned model/context selectors versus joint selection.
  10. Phase 3 worker policies: coordinator-only, fixed role/model/context tables, and ProgRouter/MasRouter-style allocation. State whether each is a faithful reproduction or a scaffold-matched analogue.
  11. Phase 4 composed controller: independently tuned timing + model + context + bounded-worker policies, under the same evidence, data, pool, and shared caps as the integrated learner.

Matched ablations

Hold state encoding and value prediction fixed across timing ablations in Phase 1. Phase 2 crosses model and context choices while fixing the graph; Phase 3 varies worker count and role/model/context assignment with a fixed merge protocol. Phase 4 compares component policies, their simple composition, and the integrated controller. Report training volume and all overhead; do not attribute representation or extra-data gains to timing.

Statistical discipline

Choose prices, budgets, margins, thresholds, and primary outcomes before test evaluation. Use repeated runs and task/repository-clustered paired confidence intervals. Report absolute success-rate loss and cost reduction, failures, and all operating points; tune only on development data.

If reporting “retain 95% or 98% of Strong’s success,” define it as a relative success-rate target, not 95% task accuracy. Do not promise exact equality or superiority in advance.

Oracle and cost

Hindsight is a diagnostic—not a deployable policy.

A request-level “perfect oracle” uses known candidate outcomes. Here the diagnostic chooses among only the sampled checkpoint actions under their stated continuation protocols. Report it separately by phase and action menu; a Phase 1 selector says nothing about the optimum worker graph.

Define the local hindsight selector

a*i = argmaxa ∈ Ai [p̂selectia − λĉselectia − ηℓ̂selectia]

Ai contains sampled feasible branches. Estimate success probability and mean resource use with repeated continuations; specify tie-breaking. Select with one repetition set and report with independent repetitions to reduce winner’s-curse bias.

Report every checkpoint, including cases where no branch succeeds. One lucky success among many rollouts is not the allocation’s expected success probability.

Keep three numbers separate

Deployment route cost: sunk prefix + selected remaining branch cost (including its transfer) + router overhead not already included. Never charge handoff twice.

Data-collection cost: actual prefix runs + all branch continuations and repetitions + snapshots, infrastructure, annotations, and evaluation. Do not sum the prefix again for each clone.

Diagnostic performance: independent outcome estimates for the selected sampled branch. A budget-constrained selector and an unconstrained success oracle are different diagnostics.

Not a global upper bound

This selector sees hindsight estimates only for the collected states and actions. It does not optimize every possible future policy, graph, model, or context packet; finite noisy estimates may even underperform another policy. Call it a sampled-branch hindsight diagnostic, not a proven full-episode oracle ceiling.

Feasibility

Practical to pilot. Not yet justified to scale.

A credible first experiment

Small action space

  • One agent, two measured model endpoints.
  • A measured continuation menu before adaptive timing.
  • Quiescent, reproducible software sandboxes.
  • Small supervised predictors before complex RL.
  • A pilot sized to measure effect and noise before committing a larger data budget.
Not yet a credible promise

Unbounded control

  • Arbitrary agent graphs and model pools.
  • Exhaustive branching across a whole episode.
  • Guaranteed frontier success at lower cost.
  • Assumed millisecond end-to-end router latency.
  • A fixed dollar budget derived from an unmeasured per-rollout price.
Measure before budgeting

Collection bill

prefix runs
+ Σ continuation trials
+ infrastructure
+ annotation / evaluation

Use pilot token/cache usage and runtimes for mean and tail estimates. Prices, model versions, repeated trials, failed branches, storage, and snapshot restoration must appear in the estimate.

Go / no-go decision

Evaluate each phase on its own incremental value and its stated dependencies. Scale a learned allocation only if it generalizes and improves terminal outcomes after routing, context-transfer, or coordination costs. A negative recovery result does not by itself invalidate context or sub-agent allocation; investigate those questions without assuming the failed component helps. Prefer simple rules where they match performance, and stop expanding the action space when its added complexity is not justified.

11

Core opportunities: context pools and sub-agents

Match resources to each role as the episode evolves.

Context allocation and sub-agent model assignment are central parts of the research agenda, scoped in Phases 2–4. MasRouter, ProgRouter, CASTER, and routed handoffs are grounding and comparators—not capabilities to claim as new.

01

Model × context configuration

A long-native-context model, a cheaper short-context model with a compact packet, and the same model with more history can yield different remaining-episode values. Hold task/scaffold fixed and separate model capability from packet length and content.

02

Preserve evidence, not just tokens

Compare raw history, structured evidence packets, retrieval, and graph handoffs. Measure omitted constraints and provenance, packet creation cost, and terminal outcomes—not only compression ratio.

03

One bounded worker

At a planning, tool-feedback, or worker-dispatch checkpoint, compare coordinator-only execution against a read-only diagnosis or test-analysis worker. Fix the role and cap its calls; test Cheap versus Strong assignment with a predefined merge protocol.

04

Per-worker model assignment

Given a fixed role, local evidence packet, dependencies, and shared remaining budget, estimate that worker’s incremental contribution to terminal success. Progress-guided worker routing already exists; isolate terminal contribution, context sufficiency, and assignment effects.

05

Limited parallelism

Only after a useful one-worker result, permit at most two workers. Measure duplicate effort, merge conflicts, shared-budget contention, and critical-path latency. Parallel wall time is not the sum of worker runtimes; billed cost generally is additive.

06

Serving-aware constraints

Record actual input/output/cache tokens, queue time, and effective context limits. For self-hosted pools, benchmark KV memory and concurrency on the target stack rather than treating a simulation’s throughput assumptions as universal.

Three different notions: a model’s native maximum context, the actual evidence packet supplied to it, and a serving pool’s configured/effective window. A larger allowed window is not automatically a larger API bill; actual token/cache usage and provider rules determine it. FleetOpt’s analytical pool results and Lost in the Middle motivate measurements, not guaranteed savings or universal claims about modern models.

Evidence first. Algorithmic novelty only if earned.

02

03
